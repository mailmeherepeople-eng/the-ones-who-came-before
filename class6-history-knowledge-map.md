# Class 6 History — Examinable Knowledge Map & Game Coverage Audit

**Source:** NCERT, *Exploring Society: India and Beyond* (Grade 6, Reprint 2026–27), Theme B — Tapestry of the Past.
Chapters pulled directly from ncert.nic.in: `fees104.pdf`, `fees105.pdf`, `fees106.pdf`.

**Purpose:** Establish every discrete fact a Class 6 student can be tested on, then determine which of them a game mechanic can actually teach. Any item we cannot cover with a mechanic is an item where the "play instead of study" claim does not hold.

---

## 0. Scope, as defined by NCERT itself

NCERT states in the Letter to the Student that **every part of the book — text, side box, image, or map — can be subject to evaluation**, with exactly five exceptions:

1. Chapter-opening quotations
2. Anything the text explicitly marks "you need not remember this"
3. Diacritical marks on Sanskrit words
4. The Introduction (page 1)
5. The Glossary

This is unusually generous to us. It means **captions, figure labels, and margin definitions are all in scope** — which is good news, because those are exactly the things a visual game teaches best. It also means the exemption clauses are worth hunting, because they legitimately shrink the syllabus. Two big ones found:

- **Ch 4, sources diagram (p67):** "You do not need to remember them all now." The full 25-node taxonomy of source types is **not** examinable. The *concept* of multiple source categories is.
- **Ch 5, Mahābhārata regions map (Fig 5.4):** "You do not need to remember those regions." ~35 place names come off the list. Only the six named in the body text stay in.

Theme B totals roughly **130 discrete examinable items** across three chapters.

### The exercise that tells us exactly what gets tested

NCERT's own true/false set for this chapter is effectively the exam blueprint. Every one of these is a discriminating fact:

- Ṛig Veda describes the entire geography of India → **False** (only the northwest, Sapta Sindhava)
- Viṣhṇu Purāṇa describes the entire Subcontinent → **True**
- In Aśhoka's time Jambudvīpa included today's India, and parts of Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Pakistan → **True**
- Mahābhārata lists many regions including Kashmir, Kutch, Kerala → **True**
- 'Hindustān' first appeared in a Greek inscription over 2,000 years ago → **False** (Persian, ~1,800 years ago)
- In ancient Persian, 'Hindu' refers to the Hindu religion → **False** (purely geographical)
- 'Bhārata' was given to India by foreign travellers → **False** (Indian origin)

**Ch 5 coverage tally:** 26 items — 🗺️ 18 · ❌ 8
**Covered by a mechanic: 69% — but only if the TRAVEL mechanic gets built. With the dig mechanic alone: 0%.**

> **This is the load-bearing finding of the whole audit.** Chapter 5 is etymology, texts, and travellers. There is nothing to excavate. A voxel dig site cannot teach that 'Indoi' came from 'Hindu' came from 'Sindhu'. If we ship only the archaeology game, Chapter 5 is entirely uncovered and the guarantee collapses.
>
> The good news: the chapter has an obvious native mechanic. **You play as an arriving outsider.** Enter from the northwest as a Persian and the first great river you cross is the Sindhu — so that is what you call the place, and your word mutates as it travels to Greece and then to Rome. Enter as Xuanzang from China and you carry a different word home along with the manuscripts. The etymology chains (5.21, 5.22) *are* journeys. Making them journeys is the whole design.

> **Design note:** 6.2–6.8 is a seven-item checklist, and it is almost certainly a long-answer question. It is also the single best argument for the city-builder mechanic: make these seven the **win conditions**. The player does not memorise the list; the player is *blocked* until each one is satisfied. NCERT even asks "which characteristic is the most fundamental — essential to the development of all others?" That is a tech-tree question. The answer the design should let players discover is productive agriculture, because nothing else unlocks without surplus food.

| # | Must know | Likely format | Coverage |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6.50 | The **Great Bath** is at **Mohenjo-daro** — a small but elaborate tank, about **⚠️12 × 7 metres** | 1-mark, near-certain | ⛏️ DIG |

> **Design note:** 6.53–6.55 is the best single scene in the entire theme. Three competing hypotheses, one eliminated by a *second* piece of evidence found elsewhere in the same city, and an explicit admission that the answer is unknown. This is the dig mechanic operating at full power: the player finds the Bath, forms a theory, then finds bathrooms in ordinary houses across town and has to *retract* it. Teaching a child that finding more evidence can destroy your own conclusion is worth more than the marks it earns.

### 4.10 Decline

| # | Must know | Likely format | Coverage |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6.91 | Around **1900 BCE** the civilisation began to fall apart; **cities were abandoned one by one** | Near-certain | 📅 TIME |
| 6.92 | Remaining inhabitants **adopted a rural lifestyle**; the earlier government or administration **no longer existed** | Short answer | 🏗️ BUILD |

| 6.95 | **Warfare and invasion are ruled out** — there is **no trace of warfare or invasion**, and the Harappans **kept no army or weapons of war**. It seems to have been a **relatively peaceful civilisation** | Long answer, very high value | ⛏️ DIG |
| 6.96 | **Agreed factor 1: climatic change** affecting much of the world from about **2000 BCE**, causing **reduced rainfall and a drier phase**, making agriculture harder and cutting food supply to the cities | Near-certain | 🏗️ BUILD |
| 6.97 | **Agreed factor 2: the Sarasvatī River dried up in its central basin**, so cities there — **Kalibangan, Banawali** — were suddenly abandoned | Near-certain | 🏗️ BUILD |
| 6.98 | Other factors may have existed; both agreed factors show **how much we depend on climate and environment** | Long answer | 🏗️ BUILD |
| 6.99 | Although the cities disappeared, **much Harappan culture and technology survived** into the next phase of Indian civilisation | 1-mark | 🏗️ BUILD |

> **Design note on 6.95:** "We know there was no war because we found no weapons" is an **argument from absence**, and it is the most sophisticated reasoning move in the entire Class 6 syllabus. In a dig game it is trivially expressible and impossible to fake: the player finishes excavating a whole city, opens the finds inventory, and the weapons category is empty. That empty slot is the lesson. No other medium can produce it as cleanly.

> **Design note on 6.96–6.97:** These two are the natural **endgame** of the builder. The player has spent the level growing a city, and then rainfall drops and the river fails, and the city cannot be saved. Losing the city *is* the correct answer to "why did the civilisation decline?" Let the player lose.

### What this says about the build

**1. The dig mechanic alone covers 29% of the theme.** It is excellent where it applies — and it applies to almost nothing in Chapter 5. Shipping only the archaeology game means the guarantee is false for a third of the syllabus.

**2. The builder is bigger than the dig.** 56 items versus 50. The Age of Empires instinct from the voice conversation was the right one, and the audit backs it: Chapter 6 is overwhelmingly about *how a city works*, not about *what was found in it*. Town planning, water management, trade, agriculture, and decline are all systems, and systems are what builders teach.

**3. Chronology's engine covers 26 items for free.** Everything in section 2.2 is timeline-ordering and BCE/CE arithmetic. That is a whole existing codebase pointed at a whole section of a chapter.

**4. Four mechanics, not one.** DIG, BUILD, TIME, TRAVEL. That is the honest minimum for the coverage claim. It is also, realistically, more than one game.

**5. The 22 uncovered items cluster tightly** — calendar systems (4.9, 4.10, 4.22, 4.23), definitions of specialists who do not dig (4.4, 4.6), and text-history in Chapter 5 (5.6, 5.10, 5.11, 5.12, 5.15, 5.17). These are the residue: facts with no world-model behind them. They will need either a fifth mechanic or an honest admission that the game covers 87% and something else covers the rest.

### Scope options

- **Vertical slice:** Chapter 6 only, DIG + BUILD. 99% coverage of one chapter, two mechanics, one dig site that becomes one city. This is the shippable 20%.
- **Full Theme B:** all four mechanics, three chapters, 87% coverage. This is a real product and a real risk.
- **Chapter 6 plus timeline:** add TIME by porting Chronology. Covers Ch 6 fully and roughly a quarter of Ch 4 for comparatively little new code.

## 6. Open items before any build

1. ⚠️ **Verify the numerals** flagged in items 6.13, 6.45, 6.50, 6.73, and the date-event pairings in Fig 4.3. The NCERT PDF's font encoding dropped digits during text extraction, and these were deliberately not filled in from coaching sites, which are frequently wrong on exactly these figures. Ten minutes with the printed pages resolves all of them.
2. **Decide whether Chapter 7 (India's Cultural Roots) is in scope.** It sits in Theme C, not Theme B, but it is history in everything but filing, and it is where the Vedas, the Buddha, and Aśhoka actually get taught. Excluding it is defensible; excluding it silently is not.
3. **Get a real CBSE Class 6 question paper.** This map predicts exam formats from the textbook's own exercises. That is a good proxy, not evidence. One real paper would let the "near-certain" tags be verified rather than estimated.
4. **Decide what the guarantee actually says.** "Students learn by playing" is defensible at 87%. "Students can answer every exam question by playing" is not, and should not be claimed unless the residue is covered.
