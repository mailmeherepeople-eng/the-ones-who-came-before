# The Ones Who Came Before: what the game is

Canon. It changes only on the user's word. Read it before touching direction, scope or priorities.

## In one line

You live as an early human and leave traces, time buries them, then you dig your own life back up as a
five-specialist team and reason about it like a historian.

## The pillars, in order

1. **The concept is the verb.** The player has to use the idea to make a decision, and the world shows why it
   matters. Pressing the highlighted thing is not enough.
2. **Runs on the floor phone.** 30 fps, a small draw-call budget, offline.
3. **Clear at every moment.** A child always knows the next step; teaching text stays until read.
4. **Look.** One committed style (Vivid, a flat storybook palette) and an Indian, period-specific land.

## The concepts

51 tracked items: the 50-item knowledge map plus 4.51 (reconstructed gender roles are possibilities). Map:
`docs/COVERAGE.md`. The big ideas:

- The past is known through sources, and historians judge sources that agree or contradict.
- Most of the past is lost: organic things rot, languages leave no trace.
- Time is counted: BCE and CE, no year zero, gaps, decades to millennia, calendars.
- Different specialists read different evidence.
- People moved from bands and camps to settled farming, villages and exchange.

## The three acts

| Act | The player | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| 1 LIVE | an early human from about 38,000 years ago to 5,400 BCE: the training cave, the band camp (gather, hunt, fish, knap, paint, beads, trade, burial), the thaw, the hamlet (plant, herd, granary, chieftain), the village (pot and maker's mark, basket, delivery, copper) | 4.32 to 4.50 |
| 2 PASS | a time dial over a diorama of the same valley: watch your hut erode, then BCE/CE, gaps, decades to millennia, calendars, deep time | 4.8 to 4.23, 4.26, 4.31, 4.40 |
| 3 DIG | a five-specialist team at the present day: survey, dig your own objects, fossils, read your own mark, interviews, claim board, lab, site report | 4.1 to 4.7, 4.24 to 4.30, 4.51 |

## The central trick

Act 1 manufactures the sources Act 3 digs up: your painting (saved verbatim), your pot's profile and mark, the
elder's grave goods. The basket you made with one effortless tap is the one thing missing ("ORGANIC: NOT
PRESERVED"). Preservation bias is taught by an absence the player feels, not a text box.

## Tone and voice

- Second person, present tense, concrete nouns, one idea per line.
- The narrator turns out to be the epigraphist: every note is her field notes.
- The tribe speaks in pictograms and noises, never words: their language is lost, which is the lesson.
- Nobody in the fiction says "quiz". Wrong answers explain and allow a retry.
- Reconstructions are hedged ("Perhaps they believed something continued"); illustrations are labelled
  illustrative; the Act 2 settlement is labelled an imagined valley.

## Hard lines

- Correct against the 2024 edition; numerals checked against the printed book. Protected phrasings stay verbatim
  (no year zero, the 2,583-year example, "Perhaps they believed", "conventional year", the Ice Age span, about
  300,000 years). Old-syllabus terms are banned (the lint lists them).
- No em or en dashes in anything the player sees.
- Minors' data: pseudonymous and on the device by default. Classroom identity (`?pilot`) is off by default and has
  no path from the title screen.
- Nobody caricatured; symbolic images are never presented as portraits or eyewitness scenes.
