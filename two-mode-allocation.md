# Two Modes: SKY and GROUND — Content Allocation

Companion to `class6-history-knowledge-map.md`. This document decides which mode teaches which item, and why.

---

## 1. The allocation rule

**Altitude equals timescale.** This is the whole principle, and everything else falls out of it.

| | GROUND (voxel, first-person) | SKY (slider, top-down) |
|---|---|---|
| Time span visible | Minutes to a single life | Centuries to hundreds of millennia |
| Scale visible | An object in your hand, a room, a street | A settlement, a river, a subcontinent |
| What it teaches | Evidence, objects, reasoning, daily life | Scale, sequence, systems, cause over time |
| Player is | A person | Nobody. A viewpoint |
| Exam shape it serves | 1-mark recall, 2-mark definitions and lists | 5-mark causal chains |

Two sentences that settle most arguments about where something goes:

> **You cannot see a grid from the ground. You cannot touch a pot from the sky.**

Chapter 6 hands us the perfect proof of each. Item 6.34 — Harappan streets were *oriented to the cardinal directions* — is literally imperceptible from inside the city; you have to go up to see it. Item 6.47 — Dholavira's largest reservoir was **33 metres** long — is meaningless from above, and NCERT's own exercise asks students to measure their classroom and compare. That one you have to *walk*.

## 2. The loop: GROUND → SKY → GROUND

The modes are not two games with a menu between them. They are one cycle, and the cycle is the argument of the chapter.

1. **GROUND.** You do things. Gather, dig, make a pot, talk to someone, plant a crop.
2. **SKY.** You spend years. You watch the consequence arrive at a scale you could never have perceived from inside it — the hamlet becomes a village, the ice retreats, the river shifts, the city empties.
3. **GROUND.** You drop back into a world that has changed because of what you did, and there are new things to do in it.

Every era transition is a handoff. That is also, not coincidentally, how the textbook is written: people do things, time passes, the world is different.

The transition verb is **zoom**. Zooming out speeds time up; zooming in slows it down. That relationship is itself one of the lessons.

The correct answer to "why did the civilisation decline" is *you watched it happen and could not stop it*. Do not give them a way to win this one.

## 5. What the mark structure implies for targeting

From the sample paper's format (which is worth keeping even though its content is not):

| Mark value | Question shape | Which mode should own it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 mark | Single recall: a date, a name, a place | Either. Sky for dates, ground for objects |
| 2 marks | A definition or a short list | **GROUND.** Definitions attach to things you have held |
| 5 marks | An enumerated causal chain — *"state at least four reasons why..."* | **SKY.** Causation over time is what the dial exists to show |

The 5-markers are where the marks concentrate and where rote study fails hardest, and they are exactly what a top-down time simulation is built to teach. That is the strongest single argument for the sky mode being a real mode rather than a tutorial screen.
